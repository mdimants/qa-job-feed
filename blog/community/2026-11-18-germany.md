## 1. r/QualityAssurance (vai r/softwaretesting)

**Title:** I analysed 141 open QA jobs in Germany. ISTQB shows up in 36% of them

**Body:**

I run a small job board for QA/testing roles, so I have the data and got curious about Germany specifically. Some numbers from the 141 German QA listings currently open (96 companies):

- Only 17% are fully remote. Most are on-site or hybrid, and the remote ones are usually limited to people living in Germany.
- 38% of descriptions explicitly ask for German.
- ISTQB is mentioned in 36% of listings. That is much higher than what I see in US or remote-first postings.
- Biggest role groups: Software Tester (32%), Test Manager (25%), QA Engineer (18%), Test Engineer (16%).
- Selenium and Playwright are almost level in German postings (21% vs 20%).
- Only 5% of listings publish a salary.

For those of you working in Germany: does ISTQB actually matter in interviews, or is it just a CV filter?

Full write-up with the method, if anyone wants it: https://www.qajobboard.com/blog/qa-jobs-in-germany-2026

## 2. r/cscareerquestionsEU

**Title:** QA/testing jobs in Germany: 83% are on-site or hybrid, 38% ask for German (data from 141 listings)

**Body:**

I see a lot of questions here about moving to Germany for tech jobs, so here is some data for the QA/testing side. I run a QA job board and looked at all 141 German QA listings we have right now:

- 17% fully remote, 83% on-site or hybrid.
- 38% ask for German language skills; roughly 1 in 5 ads are written in German.
- ISTQB Foundation is mentioned in 36% of listings, so it is worth getting if you plan to apply in Germany.
- Test Manager is a quarter of the market, which is unusual compared with other countries.
- Salaries are rarely published (5% of listings), so expect to give your "Gehaltsvorstellung" in the application.

Happy to answer questions about the data. The full breakdown is here: https://www.qajobboard.com/blog/qa-jobs-in-germany-2026
