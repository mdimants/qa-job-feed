Remote QA jobs are everywhere, but "remote" rarely means you can work from anywhere. We looked at the location rules of every remote QA, SDET and testing job on QA Job Board. Only 14 of 1,054 remote QA jobs (1.3%) accept candidates from any country. The rest are remote within one country or a short list of countries.

So the right question is not "which companies hire remote QA engineers?" It is "which companies hire remote QA engineers where I live?" Here is what the data says.

Most of the time, the restriction comes from the company's legal setup, not from a dislike of foreign applicants. Hiring in a country means paying taxes and following employment law there. That is why a US startup will often hire "remote, US only".

What remote QA employers ask for

  • Experience: only 3.9% of remote QA jobs are junior. 58% are mid-level, 23% are senior and 15% are lead or manager roles. Remote teams want people who can work on their own.
  • Automation: Selenium and Playwright are the most requested tools across all QA jobs (29% and 25% of descriptions). See which QA tools employers want.
  • Contracts: 13% of remote QA jobs are contracts. Contracts are more common outside the US, where companies hire through B2B agreements or employer-of-record services.

How to find a remote QA job you can actually get

  1. Read the location line first. "Remote (United States)" means you need US work authorization. Skip roles you cannot legally take.
  2. Search your own country first. There are 70 times more single-country remote jobs than "work from anywhere" jobs, and fewer people apply to each one.
  3. Target companies that hire in your time zone. Latin America for US companies, Eastern Europe for EU companies, India and the Philippines for global IT services.
  4. Show you can work alone. Mention clear bug reports, written test plans and CI pipelines you set up. These prove you can work async.
  5. Get alerts. New remote QA jobs fill quickly. Sign up for QA job alerts on QA Job Board.

Red flags in remote QA job posts

Remote listings attract scams and low-quality gigs. Be careful when a post:

  • asks you to pay for training, equipment or a "certification" before you start;
  • pays only per bug found, with no hourly rate or salary;
  • moves the conversation to a chat app before any video interview;
  • has no company name, website or careers page you can check;
  • promises a high salary for "simple app testing" with no experience needed.

Every company in our remote list links to a real careers page or applicant tracking system. When in doubt, apply through the company's own site.
