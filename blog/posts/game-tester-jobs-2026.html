Game tester jobs exist, but they look different from the rest of the QA market. On QA Job Board today, 25 live posts have "game", "games", "gaming" or "casino" in the title, from 12 employers. Most of them are remote (21 of 25), most are contract or freelance work (15 of 25), and almost half are localization testing (LQA) roles where the main requirement is a native language, not years of experience. Not one of the 25 posts publishes a salary. If you want a game QA job, you can get in faster than in general software testing, but you should plan for project-based work and learn to read the contract terms.

How many game tester jobs are there?

Game testing is a small slice of the board. Next to the 1,152 open QA jobs in our latest data, 25 posts with a gaming word in the title is a niche. Those extra posts are mostly general QA roles at companies that build games, gambling platforms or tools for game studios.

Who is hiring game testers?

The 25 posts come from 12 employers, and one of them dominates.

  • Testing and localization vendors. Pole To Win International has 11 of the 25 posts (44%), all of them freelance LQA or console testing roles. TransPerfect has two more freelance LQA posts, Keywords Studios has a paid on-call community tester program, and ModSquad has two support and QA roles for game development clients. Big studios often outsource testing to these companies, so a vendor is the most common first employer in game QA.
  • Game studios. Supercell (Senior Game Tester for Clash Royale, Shanghai), Ubisoft (Intern Game Tester, Chengdu), Scopely (QA Engineer and QA intern for a casino title, Bangalore) and Stillfront (freelance QA Automation Engineer for mobile games) hire testers directly.
  • iGaming and casino companies. Playson, Hard Rock Digital and Torrero hire QA engineers for online casino games. These are full-time engineering roles with the same expectations as other software QA jobs, plus domain rules around payouts and regulation.

What is LQA game testing?

Localization QA is the biggest single type of game testing job on the board: 12 of the 25 posts have "LQA" in the title. An LQA tester plays a translated version of a game and checks the text in their native language: wrong translations, text that does not fit the screen and broken characters. The current posts cover Italian, Japanese, Czech, Dutch, French, European Spanish, Latin American Spanish, Arabic, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and Malay.

The entry bar is low. One Pole To Win post says no experience is required, only fluency in the target language, conversational English, attention to detail and basic Excel skills. Many LQA posts also require you to live in a specific region, often the Schengen Area. Ten of the 25 game posts are limited to Europe. If you speak a second language well, LQA is the shortest path into the games industry. You can browse LQA jobs directly.

Remote, contract or full-time?

Game testing is more remote than you might expect. 21 of the 25 posts (84%) are remote. That is close to the market as a whole, where 90.8% of QA jobs on the board are remote.

The big difference is the type of contract. 15 of the 25 game posts (60%) are contract work, and 14 have "freelance" in the title. Across all QA jobs, only 172 of 1,152 (about 15%) are contracts. Freelance game testing usually means hourly or project pay, with work coming in waves around releases. Only 8 of the 25 game posts are full-time, and they are mostly the engineering roles at studios and iGaming companies. If you want steady remote work, look at all remote QA jobs as well, not only gaming ones.

What skills do game QA jobs ask for?

The requirements split into two groups.

Tester and LQA roles

Console and platform knowledge matters most. 14 of the 25 descriptions mention consoles, 11 mention PlayStation or PS5, and 10 mention Xbox and Nintendo Switch. Keywords Studios asks only that you are 18 or older, have a stable internet connection and own at least one gaming device. These roles also ask for clear bug reports, patience with repetitive checks and strict confidentiality: NDAs are standard, and breaking one ends the contract.

QA engineer roles

Studio and iGaming engineering roles look like regular QA jobs with a games layer on top.

  • Manual and regression testing. Playson asks for 4+ years of manual testing, test documentation, cross-browser and mobile testing, and BrowserStack. CommIT asks for 1–2 years of manual mobile testing and a test management tool such as TestRail or Qase. Torrero asks for 3+ years of manual web and mobile testing and Git.
  • Game automation. Stillfront lists Appium, AltTester, GameDriver and the Unity Test Framework, with Python or C# scripting and tests running in CI. Across the whole market, Appium appears in 96 QA job descriptions (8.7%), so it is a useful skill even outside games.
  • Client-server debugging. Stillfront and Playson both want testers who can tell a client bug from a server bug, for example by reading network traffic or logs.
  • Regulated games. Hard Rock Digital asks QA engineers to verify paytables, RTP (return to player) and payouts against the game's math model, and to help prepare evidence for regulatory certification.

How much do game testers earn?

We cannot give a game-specific salary figure. Not one of the 25 game QA posts lists a salary, so any number would be a guess. Across all QA jobs on the board, only 18.1% of posts include a salary at all.

The wider market gives a useful reference point. The median advertised QA salary is $110,000 a year (from 208 posts with a USD range). For junior roles the median is $81,000, but that is based on only 13 posts, so treat it as a rough guide. Game testing, especially freelance LQA, usually pays below these figures, and pay is often hourly. Our QA engineer salary report breaks down pay by role and seniority.

How to get a game tester job

  1. Pick a track. LQA and console testing get you in quickly. QA engineering at a studio or iGaming company pays more and offers full-time contracts, but needs 1–4 years of testing experience.
  2. Use your languages. If you are fluent in a second language, apply for LQA roles first. They make up nearly half of current game QA posts.
  3. Write good bug reports. Practice on games you play: steps to reproduce, expected and actual result, platform and a short video.
  4. Learn one automation tool. Appium or the Unity Test Framework moves you from tester to QA engineer, which is where full-time game QA jobs are.
  5. Do not limit yourself to games. Mobile, web and payments testing use the same skills. Many testers start in general QA and move into games later. Junior QA engineer jobs and our guide on how to get your first QA job are good starting points.
