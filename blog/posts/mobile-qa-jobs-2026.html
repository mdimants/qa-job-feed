Mobile testing is a large part of the QA market, but few jobs carry "mobile" in the title. In our snapshot, Appium appears in 97 QA job posts (8.6% of posts with a full description), Espresso in 26 and XCUITest in 18. When we searched the full job file more widely, 322 posts asked for mobile app testing in some form, yet only 48 had "mobile", "iOS", "Android" or "Appium" in the job title. If you only search for "mobile QA", you miss most of these roles. Most of them are listed as QA Engineer or QA Automation Engineer, they are mostly remote, and where they publish pay, the median is a little above the QA market as a whole.

How many mobile QA jobs are there?

QA Job Board had 1,171 open QA jobs from 788 companies on 27 November 2026. For a closer look we used the public job file at qajobboard.com/jobs.json, kept the 1,862 jobs posted since 1 October 2026 that have a full description, and looked for mobile testing signals: Appium, Espresso, XCUITest, Detox, Maestro, or phrases such as "mobile testing", "mobile app" and "iOS application".

That gave us 322 posts (17.3%) that ask for mobile testing work. They come from 225 different companies. The biggest single poster is Testlio, a crowdtesting company, with 18 posts. After that, no company has more than 5.

Only 48 of those jobs say "mobile", "iOS", "Android" or "Appium" in the title. By category:

  • QA Engineer: 82 posts
  • QA Automation Engineer: 66 posts
  • Senior QA Engineer: 46 posts
  • Software Tester: 36 posts
  • SDET: 17 posts

The practical advice: search for the tool, not the title. Browse Appium jobs or search all mobile QA jobs, then filter by role on the QA automation engineer jobs page.

A few things stand out. First, cross-platform tools win. Appium and WebdriverIO run one test suite against both Android and iOS, which is what most teams want. 124 of the mobile posts mention both iOS and Android, so employers usually expect you to test on both platforms, not just one.

Second, Espresso and XCUITest are tied to native Android and iOS code, so they tend to appear in roles close to app developers. If you know Kotlin or Swift, these jobs are a good fit. Kotlin appears in 25 of the mobile posts and Swift in 14.

Cloud device farms matter too: BrowserStack and Sauce Labs appear in 36 and 23 mobile posts, and 31 posts mention real or physical devices directly.

Mobile QA is rarely mobile-only

The most useful finding for job seekers is how much web and API testing these roles also ask for. In the 322 mobile posts:

  • Selenium: 135 posts (41.9%)
  • Postman: 117 posts (36.3%)
  • Playwright: 112 posts (34.8%)
  • Cypress: 85 posts (26.4%)
  • REST Assured: 51 posts (15.8%)
  • CI tools (Jenkins, GitHub Actions, GitLab, Bitrise, CircleCI): 89 posts (27.6%)

Selenium shows up in almost as many mobile posts as Android does (135 vs 141). That makes sense: Appium uses the WebDriver protocol, so teams that already run Selenium often add Appium for their apps. A typical mobile QA job covers web, mobile and API testing. If you want to compare the web tools, see our analysis of Playwright vs Selenium vs Cypress.

For programming languages, Python appears in 98 mobile posts, Java in 91, JavaScript in 91 and TypeScript in 62. Across the whole board, Python leads with 25.1% of posts, so mobile roles follow the same pattern. Java stays strong here because of Appium Java clients and Android code.

How much do mobile QA engineers earn?

Only 39 of the 322 mobile posts publish a salary in US dollars, so treat these numbers as a guide, not a benchmark. Using the midpoint of each annual range, the median is about $119,000, with the middle half between $101,000 and $140,000.

For comparison, the median advertised salary across all QA jobs on QA Job Board is $111,000 (233 posts with pay), and the QA Automation Engineer median is $116,000. So mobile testing skills do not create a big premium on their own, but they place you in the upper part of the QA pay range, close to automation and SDET roles (SDET median: $121,000).

Some examples from recent posts: a Lead Mobile QA Automation Engineer role at Unanet lists a midpoint of about $135,000, a QA Engineer role for wearables, mobile and cloud at Urrly lists $142,500, and a QA Engineer, Mobile role at GN Group lists $101,000. All three are remote in the US.

Seniority: few junior roles

Mobile QA is not an easy entry point. Based on words in the title, only 3 of the 322 posts were clearly junior or internship roles, while 121 were senior, lead, staff or principal. The rest were mid-level. Across the whole board, 51 of 1,171 jobs are junior, so junior roles are scarce everywhere, but even more so in mobile.

If you are starting out, a general QA role that covers web and mobile is a more realistic first step. Crowdtesting platforms such as Testlio can also give you real device experience for your CV.

How to get a mobile QA job in 2026

  1. Learn Appium first. It covers almost half of mobile posts and works on both platforms. Pair it with the language you already know: Python, Java or JavaScript.
  2. Keep your web and API skills. Selenium or Playwright plus Postman appear in a large share of mobile posts. Do not drop them.
  3. Add one native framework. Espresso if you lean Android, XCUITest if you lean iOS. This opens the native-team roles.
  4. Show real device work. Run your test suite on BrowserStack or Sauce Labs (both offer free trials) and in a CI pipeline, and link it from your CV.
  5. Search by tool. Most mobile QA jobs do not say "mobile" in the title. Search for Appium, Espresso and "mobile" separately.

Frequently asked questions

How many mobile QA jobs are there in 2026?

In our November 2026 data, 322 of 1,862 QA job posts with a description (17.3%) asked for mobile app testing. Only 48 of them had "mobile", "iOS", "Android" or "Appium" in the title. The rest were listed as QA Engineer, QA Automation Engineer or similar roles.

Is Appium still in demand?

Yes. Appium appears in 97 open QA job posts on QA Job Board (8.6% of posts with a description) and in 46.6% of the mobile testing posts we analysed. No other mobile testing tool comes close.

How much does a mobile QA engineer earn?

The median advertised salary for mobile QA posts was about $119,000 a year, based on 39 posts with a USD salary. That is a little above the $111,000 median for all QA jobs on the board. The sample is small, so treat it as a guide.

Should I learn Espresso and XCUITest or just Appium?

Start with Appium, because it appears in far more posts. Espresso (32 mobile posts) and XCUITest (28) are worth adding if you want to work in a native Android or iOS team, where tests are usually written in Kotlin or Swift.

Are mobile QA jobs remote?

Mostly yes. 88.5% of the mobile testing posts we analysed were remote, close to the 91.5% remote share across all QA jobs on the board. Many remote roles are still limited to one country, most often the United States.

Methodology

Board-wide figures (1,171 open QA jobs, 788 companies, tool shares for Appium, Espresso and XCUITest, the 91.5% remote share and salary medians) come from QA Job Board data collected on 27 November 2026. Mobile figures come from the public listings at qajobboard.com/jobs.json on the same day: we kept the 1,862 jobs posted since 1 October 2026 with a full description and found 322 that mention mobile testing tools or phrases, using keyword matching on titles and descriptions. Salaries use the midpoint of each published USD range converted to annual pay, and seniority is based on words in the job title.
