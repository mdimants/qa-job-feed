Germany is one of the bigger QA job markets in Europe, but it works differently from the remote-first market you see in US job ads. Right now QA Job Board lists 141 QA and testing jobs in Germany from 96 companies. Most of them are on-site or hybrid, many expect some German, and the ISTQB certificate shows up far more often than in other countries. If you know those three things before you apply, your search gets much faster.

How many QA jobs are open in Germany?

On 18 November 2026 the board had 141 open QA roles located in Germany, posted by 96 different employers. No single company dominates: the largest employers in the list (SINC, Bundesdruckerei and Helsing) have four open roles each. That is a good sign for job seekers, because it means the demand is spread across banks, insurers, software houses, aerospace start-ups, medical device makers and public-sector IT providers.

For comparison, the board as a whole tracks 996 fresh QA jobs from 692 companies in today's feed, and 222 of them were added in the last 7 days. Germany is a steady part of that flow rather than a short-term spike.

Remote, hybrid or on-site?

This is where Germany differs most. Across the whole board, 91.8% of today's QA jobs are remote. In Germany only 24 of 141 jobs (17%) are fully remote; the other 83% are on-site or hybrid, so you are expected to live within reach of the office.

The remote roles that do exist are usually limited to people living in Germany ("Deutschlandweit remote"), because of employment contracts and tax rules. If you live elsewhere in Europe and want to work for German companies, the general remote QA jobs list is a better place to start. Do you need to speak German?

Often, yes. We searched the 140 German job descriptions that include full text:

  • 53 of 140 (38%) explicitly mention German language skills, for example "sehr gute Deutschkenntnisse" or "fluent German".
  • Roughly 1 in 5 descriptions appear to be written in German, which usually means German is the working language of the team.
  • 75% of job titles use the German "(m/w/d)" or "(m/f/d)" format, which is a legal convention in Germany and tells you the ad was written for the German market.

English-only roles exist mainly in start-ups, scale-ups and international product companies (payments, gaming, quantum computing, defence tech). If your German is basic, filter for listings written in English and mention your current level honestly in your CV, for example "German B1, improving". Many employers are fine with that for technical roles.

What do QA jobs in Germany pay?

Honest answer: German employers rarely publish salaries. Only 7 of the 141 German listings (5%) include a salary, which is too few for a reliable median. The full-time annual ranges that were published had midpoints between €45,000 and €80,000. Across the whole board, 17.9% of jobs publish pay and the median advertised QA salary is $110,000, but that figure is driven mostly by US roles.

In practice, the German offer depends heavily on city, industry and whether the role is test management or automation. Ask about the range in the first call; it is normal in Germany to give your "Gehaltsvorstellung" (salary expectation) in the application. How to get hired faster in Germany

  1. Use a German-style CV. Two pages, clear dates, a short skills block and, for German-language ads, a German version. Add a professional photo only if you are comfortable with it; it is common but not required.
  2. Get ISTQB Foundation Level if you do not have it. It is mentioned in 36% of German listings.
  3. Show one automation project. A small Playwright or Selenium framework on GitHub with CI is enough to stand out for QA Engineer roles.
  4. State your German level clearly (A2, B1, C1). Recruiters filter on it, and vague wording costs interviews.
  5. Look beyond Germany too. Austria and Switzerland hire for the same skills and often accept German-speaking candidates from abroad: QA jobs in Austria, QA jobs in Switzerland.

Frequently asked questions

How many QA jobs are there in Germany right now?

QA Job Board lists 141 open QA and testing jobs in Germany from 96 companies (November 2026). The list is updated daily with new roles from German employers and job sources.

Can I work as a QA engineer in Germany without speaking German?

Yes, but your options are narrower. 38% of German QA job descriptions explicitly ask for German, so English-only candidates should focus on start-ups and international product companies that write their ads in English.

Is ISTQB required for QA jobs in Germany?

It is not always required, but it helps a lot. ISTQB is mentioned in 36% of German QA listings, much more often than in other markets, and it is frequently expected for test manager roles.

Are there remote QA jobs in Germany?

Some. Only 17% of QA jobs in Germany are fully remote, and most of those are limited to people who live in Germany. The rest are on-site or hybrid, so plan to live near the office.
