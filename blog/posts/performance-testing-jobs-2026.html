Performance testing is a smaller part of the QA job market than web automation, but it is far from a niche. JMeter appears in 118 QA job posts on QA Job Board (10.6% of posts with a full description), k6 in 67 (6.0%) and Gatling in 44 (4.0%). Only 20 open jobs carry a dedicated "performance tester" title, though. Most load testing work is done by automation engineers and SDETs who list JMeter or k6 next to Selenium, Playwright and an API tool. The posts that mention a load testing tool are almost all remote and, where they publish pay, sit above the QA median.

Dedicated performance tester roles are rare

This is the most important thing to understand about the market. Our role classification puts only 20 of 1,152 open QA jobs in the Performance Tester category, and in the job file just 19 posts have "performance" or "load test" in the title. Examples include Performance Test Engineer roles at Veracode, Yum! Brands and Pizza Hut, a Senior Performance Engineer at Redis, a Staff Performance Engineer on Mozilla's JavaScript engine and a Sr SDET focused on performance at HighLevel.

If you want to specialise in performance, apply to the dedicated roles on the performance tester jobs page, but do not wait for them. The bigger pool is QA automation engineer jobs and SDET jobs where load testing is one of the responsibilities.

JMeter vs k6 vs Gatling: which should you learn?

Of the 146 posts, 57 mention just one performance tool and 89 mention two or more, usually in a line like "experience with JMeter, k6 or Gatling". Only 9 posts mention k6 as the only performance tool, while 33 mention only JMeter.

Remote, mid-level and senior

142 of the 146 performance tool posts (97.3%) are remote, above the 90.8% remote share across all QA jobs. Most are full-time (135 posts); 9 are contracts and 2 are internships. By job title, 66 posts are mid-level, 53 senior and 22 lead or manager roles. Only 5 are junior, which is too small a group to draw conclusions from, but it confirms that employers treat performance testing as a skill for experienced testers.

The posts come from 119 different companies. Upgrade has the most (5), followed by Consultec-TI, BTSE and DecisionPoint Corporation with 3 each. Where a remote post limits location, the most common are the United States (52 posts), India (15) and Canada (10), with Brazil, Poland and Mexico at 5 each. How much do performance testing jobs pay?

Only 22 of the 146 posts publish a USD salary, so treat this as a rough signal. Their median is $131,500 a year, with the middle half between about $116,000 and $142,500. For comparison, the median across all QA jobs with a published salary is $110,000 (208 salaries), QA Automation Engineer posts have a median of $115,000 and Senior QA Engineer posts $127,000.

The premium fits: these posts are more senior and ask for more infrastructure skills than the average QA job. Too few dedicated performance tester roles publish pay for a separate median. Only 18.1% of all QA posts include a salary, so ask early. Our QA engineer salary guide breaks pay down by role and seniority.

How to get a performance testing job

  1. Start from automation. Most of these jobs are automation or SDET roles. Strong Selenium or Playwright skills plus one load tool beat load testing alone.
  2. Learn JMeter, then k6. JMeter covers the most posts; k6 fits JavaScript and TypeScript teams and is growing in modern stacks.
  3. Test APIs, not just pages. Build a load test against a public or self-hosted API and show response times, error rates and throughput.
  4. Show the monitoring side. Send k6 or JMeter results to Grafana and write a short report explaining a bottleneck. This is what separates a load tester from a performance engineer.
  5. Run tests in CI. Add a small load test to a GitHub Actions or Jenkins pipeline with a pass or fail threshold. Jenkins appears in 37.0% of these posts.

If you are moving into this from an SDET path, our guide to remote SDET jobs covers the companies hiring those roles.

Frequently asked questions

Is JMeter still in demand in 2026?

Yes. JMeter is mentioned in 118 QA job posts on QA Job Board, 10.6% of posts with a full description, which makes it the most requested performance testing tool. It is often listed next to k6 or Gatling as an alternative.

How much does a performance tester earn?

QA posts that mention a performance testing tool and publish a USD salary have a median of $131,500 a year, based on 22 salaries. That is above the $110,000 median for all QA jobs, but the sample is small, so treat it as a rough guide.

Should I learn JMeter or k6?

JMeter appears in more job posts: 118 against 67 for k6. Learn k6 if you already write tests in JavaScript or TypeScript, since 71.6% of k6 posts in our sample mention TypeScript.

Are performance testing jobs remote?

Almost all of them are. 97.3% of posts that mention a load testing tool are remote, compared with 90.8% of all QA jobs, although many limit applicants to countries such as the United States, India or Canada.

Is performance testing a good career path for QA engineers?

It is a good specialisation, but few jobs are performance-only: just 20 of 1,152 open QA jobs are dedicated performance tester roles. Most demand comes from automation and SDET jobs that include load testing.

Where can I find performance testing jobs?

QA Job Board lists performance testing jobs from 119 companies, updated daily. Browse performance tester jobs or search for Gatling, JMeter and k6 jobs.

Methodology

Market-wide figures (1,152 open QA jobs, tool shares for JMeter, k6 and Gatling, the 20 Performance Tester jobs, 90.8% remote share and salary medians) come from QA Job Board data collected on 24 November 2026. Role, seniority, location, co-skill, language and salary figures for performance testing come from the same day's published job file (1,093 jobs, 1,067 with a description, 146 posts mentioning JMeter, k6, Gatling, Locust, LoadRunner, NeoLoad or BlazeMeter), using keyword matching on titles and descriptions. Salary figures use the midpoint of each USD range, converted to annual pay.
