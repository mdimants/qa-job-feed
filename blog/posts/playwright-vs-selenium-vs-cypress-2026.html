Short answer: Selenium is still mentioned in more QA job posts than any other framework, but Playwright is close behind and already leads in automation and SDET roles. Cypress is a clear third. Across 1,046 QA job posts with a full description, Selenium appears in 30.4%, Playwright in 27.0% and Cypress in 18.7%. If you can only learn one framework this year, the data points to Playwright for new automation roles and Selenium for enterprise and Java teams. Below is where each one wins, what it pays and how employers combine them.

Many of those are manual testing, QA analyst, compliance or test management roles where the framework is not the point. If you are aiming at those jobs, the framework choice matters less than test design, SQL and domain knowledge.

Do employers want one framework or several?

Usually several. Of the posts that mention at least one of the three frameworks, many name two or all three, often written as "Playwright, Cypress or Selenium". In the same-day file:

  • 160 posts mention both Playwright and Selenium.
  • 146 posts mention both Playwright and Cypress.
  • 110 posts mention all three.

Cypress almost never appears alone. Only 7.9% of Cypress posts mention neither Playwright nor Selenium. For Playwright that figure is 28.7% and for Selenium 38.4%. In practice this means Cypress is rarely a hard requirement; employers list it as one acceptable option. Selenium-only posts are the most common single-framework posts, usually in Java teams. Which one should you learn?

  1. You are starting out: learn Playwright with TypeScript. It is the framework most often requested in new automation roles, and the skills (locators, fixtures, API calls, CI) transfer to Cypress easily. Keep an eye on junior posts, which still mention Selenium more often.
  2. You work in Java: stay strong in Selenium, then add Playwright for Java. Many Java teams are planning or running a migration, and having done one is a good interview story.
  3. You already know Cypress: you are well placed for JavaScript-heavy front-end teams. Add Playwright, because 77.2% of Cypress posts also list it.
  4. Whichever you pick: add API testing and a GitHub Actions or Jenkins pipeline to your project. Then prepare for questions on waits, flaky tests and framework design with our QA automation interview questions.

Frequently asked questions

Is Playwright replacing Selenium?

Not yet, but it is getting close. Selenium is mentioned in 30.4% of QA job posts on QA Job Board and Playwright in 27.0%, and in automation and SDET roles Playwright already leads with 51.8% against 45.3%.

Should I learn Playwright, Selenium or Cypress first?

Playwright is the best first choice for most new automation engineers, because it appears in 51.8% of automation and SDET posts. If you work in Java or apply to large enterprise teams, Selenium is still the safer choice, since 41.6% of Selenium posts mention Java.

Which test automation framework pays the most?

The differences between them are small. Posts that mention Selenium have a median salary of $108,000, Playwright $105,000 and Cypress $102,000, and those groups are too small for the difference to matter. Role and seniority have a much bigger effect.

Is Cypress still in demand in 2026?

Yes, but as a third option. Cypress appears in 18.7% of QA job posts, and only 7.9% of Cypress posts mention neither Playwright nor Selenium, so employers usually accept it alongside the other two.

Methodology

We analysed 1,075 open QA jobs collected by QA Job Board on 17 November 2026; framework shares are based on the 1,046 posts with a full description, and the 6 November figures come from that day's saved dataset. Breakdowns by role, seniority, language, co-skill, country and salary come from the same day's published job file (1,006 posts with a description), using the same keyword matching. Salaries were converted to annual USD, and medians are only shown for groups with at least 5 salaries.
