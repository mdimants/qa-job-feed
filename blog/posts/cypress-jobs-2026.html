Based on 1,146 open QA jobs on QA Job Board, November 2026.

Cypress is the third most requested test automation framework in QA job posts, after Selenium and Playwright. It appears in 192 of the 1,098 QA job posts with a full description on QA Job Board (17.5%). Almost every Cypress job is remote, most are mid-level or senior automation roles, and the median advertised salary is $110,000 a year. The catch is that Cypress is rarely the only tool an employer asks for: in more than three out of four Cypress posts, Playwright is listed too.

How many QA jobs ask for Cypress?

Across all QA jobs we track, three web frameworks dominate. Selenium is mentioned in 330 posts (30.1%), Playwright in 277 (25.2%) and Cypress in 192 (17.5%). The next browser tool, WebdriverIO, appears in only 20 posts (1.8%), so if you are choosing a JavaScript framework for web testing, the real choice is between Cypress and Playwright.

Which roles ask for Cypress?

Cypress is mostly an automation engineer's tool. Among the 187 Cypress posts:

  • QA Automation Engineer: 56 posts (29.9%)
  • QA Engineer: 49 posts (26.2%)
  • Senior QA Engineer: 26 posts (13.9%)
  • SDET: 24 posts (12.8%)
  • QA Manual Tester: 9 posts (4.8%)
  • QA Analyst: 7 posts (3.7%)

Automation and SDET titles together make up 42.8% of Cypress posts. The nine manual tester posts usually list Cypress as a plus, not a requirement, which is a useful signal if you are a manual tester planning your first step into automation. The most direct places to look are QA automation engineer jobs and SDET jobs.

Seniority: almost no junior Cypress jobs

Based on job titles, 105 Cypress posts (56.1%) are mid-level, 56 (29.9%) are senior and 24 (12.8%) are lead or manager roles. Only 2 posts are junior. That is too small a group to say much about, but it matches the wider market: across all QA jobs, only 42 of 1,146 posts are junior.

The practical reading is that employers who list Cypress expect you to maintain or extend an existing test suite on your own. If you are new to testing, Cypress is still a good tool to learn, because its test runner makes it easy to see what your test is doing. Just use it in portfolio projects and apply to junior roles, as described in our junior QA roadmap.

Cypress jobs are almost all remote

185 of the 187 Cypress posts (98.9%) are remote. Across all QA jobs the remote share is 90.7%, so Cypress roles are even more remote than the QA market as a whole. That fits the kind of company that uses Cypress: web product teams, often SaaS, with JavaScript front ends and distributed engineering teams.

The posts come from 166 different companies, so no single employer shapes these numbers. Flosum has the most Cypress posts (6), followed by SupplyHouse.com, Pavago and Huzzle with 3 each.

Remote does not always mean worldwide. Among Cypress posts that limit where you can live, the most common locations are:

  • United States: 46 posts
  • India: 25 posts
  • Canada: 13 posts
  • Poland: 10 posts
  • Brazil: 9 posts
  • Mexico: 9 posts
  • United Kingdom: 8 posts

Latin America shows up more than you might expect: Brazil, Mexico, Colombia and posts open to "LATAM" add up to 30 location tags. Many US companies hire Cypress testers in nearby time zones. Use the remote QA jobs page to filter by your location, or see QA jobs in Canada and QA jobs in Poland.

Cypress almost never appears alone

This is the most useful number in the whole analysis. Of the 187 Cypress posts, 145 (77.5%) also mention Playwright and 142 (75.9%) also mention Selenium. Only 8 posts (4.3%) mention Cypress without either of the other two.

In practice, most of these posts say something like "experience with Cypress, Playwright or Selenium". Employers want someone who understands browser automation, waits, selectors and test design, and they accept any of the three. Very few jobs require Cypress specifically. If you only know Cypress, you can apply to most of these jobs, but adding Playwright is the fastest way to cover nearly all of them. Which programming language?

Cypress tests are written in JavaScript or TypeScript, and the job posts show it:

  • JavaScript: 50.8% of Cypress posts
  • TypeScript: 39.0%
  • Python: 34.8%
  • Java: 34.2%
  • C#: 10.7%

Across all QA jobs, JavaScript appears in only 21.8% of posts and TypeScript in 15.1%, so Cypress roles are much more JavaScript-heavy than the market. Python and Java still show up in about a third of posts, usually for backend or API tests that sit next to the Cypress suite. If you want to see which companies mention TypeScript, try a TypeScript search.

How much do Cypress jobs pay?

35 Cypress posts publish a salary we could convert to annual USD. Their median is $110,000, with the middle half between about $91,000 and $138,000. That is close to the overall QA median of $112,000 (based on 212 salaries).

The tool does not change pay much; the role does. Across all QA jobs, QA Automation Engineer posts have a median of $116,000 and SDET posts $130,000. The Cypress sample is too small to split by role with confidence, so treat role-level Cypress salaries as unknown. Only 18.5% of QA posts publish a salary at all, so ask about pay early in the process. Our QA engineer salary guide has more detail.

How to get a Cypress job

  1. Learn Playwright next to Cypress. 77.5% of Cypress posts also mention Playwright, and many teams are moving between the two.
  2. Write your tests in TypeScript. JavaScript and TypeScript appear in most Cypress posts, and TypeScript shows you can work in a typed codebase.
  3. Add API tests. Half of the posts ask for Postman. Show that you can test an API with cy.request inside Cypress and in Postman.
  4. Run your tests in CI. Put a public project on GitHub with a GitHub Actions workflow that runs the Cypress suite on every push.
  5. Prepare for framework questions. Our QA automation interview questions cover waits, flaky tests and framework choice.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cypress still in demand in 2026?

Yes. Cypress is mentioned in 17.5% of QA job posts on QA Job Board, which makes it the third most requested framework after Selenium and Playwright. Most of these posts also accept Playwright or Selenium.

How much does a Cypress tester earn?

QA posts that mention Cypress and publish a salary have a median of $110,000 a year, based on 35 salaries. That is close to the median for all QA jobs, because pay depends more on the role and seniority than on the tool.

Should I learn Cypress or Playwright?

If you can learn only one, Playwright appears in more job posts: 25.2% of QA posts against 17.5% for Cypress. But 77.5% of Cypress posts also mention Playwright, so knowing both covers almost every web automation job.

Are Cypress jobs remote?

Almost all of them are. 98.9% of Cypress posts on QA Job Board are remote, higher than the 90.7% share across all QA jobs, although many limit applicants to countries such as the United States, India or Canada.

What language do I need for Cypress jobs?

JavaScript or TypeScript. JavaScript appears in 50.8% of Cypress posts and TypeScript in 39.0%, compared with 21.8% and 15.1% across all QA jobs.

Methodology

Market-wide shares come from 1,146 open QA jobs collected by QA Job Board on 23 November 2026, with tool shares based on the 1,098 posts with a full description. Role, seniority, location, co-skill, language and salary figures for Cypress come from the same day's published job file (1,090 jobs, at most six per company, 187 Cypress posts), using keyword matching on the title and description. Salaries were converted to annual USD at approximate rates.
