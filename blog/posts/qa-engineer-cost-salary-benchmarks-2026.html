Based on 1,171 open QA jobs on QA Job Board, November 2026.

A mid-level QA engineer costs about $102,000 a year in base salary, going by what employers advertise right now. Half of all mid-level posts sit between $75,000 and $117,000. A senior QA engineer is advertised at a median of $116,000 and a QA lead or manager at $140,000. Those figures lean heavily on US job posts. If you hire outside the United States, the advertised numbers are much lower, and far fewer employers publish them at all. This guide gives you the benchmarks by level, role, region and skill, so you can set a budget before you write the job post.

The short version: what employers advertise

On November 27, 2026, QA Job Board listed 1,171 open QA and software testing jobs from 788 companies. 91.5% of them are remote. Only 19.9% of the posts publish a salary, which leaves 233 posts with a usable figure. Converted to US dollars per year, they look like this:

These are base salaries from job posts. Your full cost is higher once you add employer taxes, benefits, equipment and recruiting time. Those extras differ a lot by country and company, so we do not put a number on them here.

United States benchmarks

Most of the salary data comes from the US, so it is worth looking at US-only posts on their own. We counted 363 listings limited to candidates in the United States. 172 of them publish pay, which is 47%. That is far above the overall rate, because several US states require a salary range in the job post.

Outside the United States

Here the data gets thin, and that is a finding in itself. Of 521 listings limited to countries other than the US, only 32 publish a salary. That is 6%. The 32 posts have a median of $65,000, and half of them fall between $40,000 and $95,000.

  • Canada: 14 of 53 listings show pay. The median is $85,000, with half between $62,000 and $99,000.
  • Europe: 11 of 193 listings show pay. The median is $67,000, with half between $51,000 and $97,000. Eleven posts is a small sample across many countries, so use it only as a starting point.
  • India: 5 of 96 listings show pay, clustered around $16,000. Five posts cannot support a benchmark.
  • Latin America: only 2 of 79 listings show pay, so we do not report a figure.

The practical lesson for employers outside the US: almost nobody in your market shows a number. A post with a clear range will be one of very few, and candidates notice. If you hire across borders, the remote QA jobs list shows how other employers describe location limits and pay.

A programming language raises the price more than a test framework does. Posts that mention Python pay a median of $125,000, which is $14,000 above the overall figure. In US-only posts the gap is wider: $135,000 with Python (47 posts) against $115,000 without (125 posts). Posts that mention Jira pay less than average, most likely because Jira shows up most in manual and analyst roles.

This is a correlation. Python does not make a tester more expensive by itself. It marks roles where the tester writes real code, builds frameworks and works inside the engineering team. If that is what you need, budget for it. You can see how those roles are written by searching QA jobs that ask for Python.

How wide should your salary range be?

205 of the listings publish a range with a bottom and a top figure. In the median range, the top is 30% above the bottom. Half of all ranges have a spread between 19% and 50%. A range of $90,000 to $117,000 is normal. A range of $70,000 to $160,000 tells candidates you have not decided what level you are hiring.

The distribution of the midpoints is also useful for a sanity check. 40 of the 215 posts (19%) have a midpoint of $150,000 or more, and 23 (11%) are below $60,000. If your budget is in either tail, check that the title and level in your post match it.

Contract hires

161 of the 1,171 open jobs (about 14%) are contracts. We cannot give you a contract rate benchmark, because only 4 contract listings publish pay. If you plan to hire a contractor, expect to negotiate without public reference points, and ask candidates for their rate early.

How to set your budget in four steps

  1. Pick the level first. 682 of the 1,171 open jobs (58%) are mid-level. That is where most employers hire, and where the $102,000 median applies.
  2. Pick the region. For a US hire, use the US table. For other regions, start from the $65,000 non-US median and adjust with local knowledge, because public data is scarce.
  3. Add for coding skills. If the role needs Python or Java and framework work, move toward the 75th percentile for the level.
  4. Publish the range. Keep the top about 30% above the bottom. Among lead and manager roles, 33% of listings already show pay. Among mid-level roles it is 16%, so a mid-level post with a range stands out more.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to hire a QA engineer?

A mid-level QA engineer is advertised at a median of $102,000 a year, based on 105 job posts that publish pay. Half of those posts fall between $75,000 and $117,000. The figures lean toward US salaries, and your full cost is higher once taxes and benefits are added.

How much does a senior QA engineer cost?

Senior QA roles are advertised at a median of $116,000 across all regions. In US-only posts the median is $128,000, with a typical posted range of $104,000 to $150,000. Lead and manager roles have a median of $140,000.

Is a QA automation engineer more expensive than a manual tester?

Yes. QA Automation Engineer posts have a median of $116,000, against $98,000 for posts titled QA Engineer and $91,000 for QA Analyst. Posts that mention Python pay a median of $125,000.

How much does a QA engineer cost outside the United States?

The 32 non-US posts that publish pay have a median of $65,000. Canada has a median of $85,000 from 14 posts, and Europe $67,000 from 11 posts. These samples are small, because only 6% of non-US listings show a salary.

Should I put a salary range in a QA job post?

Yes. Only 19.9% of QA job posts publish a salary, so a post with a range stands out. Keep the top of the range about 30% above the bottom, which is the median spread in current posts.

Methodology

Headline counts and the level and role tables come from the QA Job Board dataset of 1,171 open jobs on November 27, 2026, of which 233 publish a salary. The US, non-US, skill and range-width figures were calculated with Python from the 1,106 listings in the published job feed, of which 215 show a salary. Salaries are the midpoint of the posted range, converted to US dollars per year at approximate exchange rates, and they are advertised figures, not what people were finally paid.
